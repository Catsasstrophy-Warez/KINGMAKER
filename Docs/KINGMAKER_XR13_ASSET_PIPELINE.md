# Kingmaker XR-13 asset pipeline

This is the production handoff for the RealityKit assembly. The runtime deliberately expects an assembly, not one monolithic car mesh.

## Current status (authoritative)

The repository currently ships one procedurally modeled, UV-mapped, textured
blockout at Sources/DHPresentation/Resources/Kingmaker_XR13.usdz. The app now
resolves that bundled asset through DHRev10AssetResolver; the diagnostic
marker hierarchy remains the fallback when a production asset is unavailable.
The Blender generator is reproducible from any checkout:

    blender --background --factory-startup --python Tools/BlenderAssetGen/build_kingmaker.py

It writes intermediate files under the ignored
Tools/BlenderAssetGen/generated/ directory and the USDZ into the package
resource directory. Use --output-dir to export to another resource folder.
This remains blockout-tier art: it is not a replacement for authored production
meshes, blend shapes, final materials, interiors, audio, VFX, or device QA.

The three owner-provided reference sheets in ResearchLibrary/ReferenceImages/
are the visual source of truth. The older calibration notes below are
historical iteration notes, not current requirements.

## Historical iteration log

A modeled **blockout** USDZ exists at `Sources/DHPresentation/Resources/Kingmaker_XR13.usdz` (bundled via `Package.swift`'s `DHPresentation` target resources), generated headlessly in Blender via `Tools/BlenderAssetGen/build_kingmaker.py` (run with `blender --background --python Tools/BlenderAssetGen/build_kingmaker.py`; regenerates the `.blend` and re-exports the `.usdz` in place). It was built directly against this file's visual canon and multi-view spec (`ResearchLibrary/KINGMAKER_VISUAL_CANON.md`, `ResearchLibrary/KINGMAKER_MULTI_VIEW_VISUAL_SPEC.md`), not as generic primitives: a lofted low-poly fastback body with a long hood and pronounced flared haunches at both wheel arches, a distinct raised fastback greenhouse volume (tinted, not body-colored, stepped up from the beltline) rather than an open notch, a hood scoop over the supercharger, front splitter and push bar, rear diffuser fins, an active rear wing, headlights/taillights, big wheels with a visible rotor+caliper inside the rim, a roll cage/pedal box/dashboard in the cabin, and a roof cargo rack — matching the sub-assembly separation `ResearchLibrary/KINGMAKER_PARTS_DISASSEMBLY_REFERENCE.md` requires, plus a slatted grille mesh and door seam lines for additional part separation. Proportions (roofline height/length, wheel/rim-to-tire ratio, rear treatment) were calibrated against the three owner-provided sheets in `ResearchLibrary/ReferenceImages/` (the canonical filenames are `XR13_OrthogonalsAndWasteland.png`, `XR13_DisassemblyAndComponentBreakdown.png`, and `XR13_ParkDisassembly_DamagedRustedBrokenRepaired.png`): a lower and longer fastback greenhouse, low-profile tires with a larger visible rim, a small integrated decklid lip, quad taillights, dual exhaust tips, and a deep dark grille opening. It follows the `XR13_Assembly` hierarchy below with proportions matched to the runtime marker geometry in `Rev10RealityKitScene.swift`. Every mesh is UV-unwrapped (Blender's smart UV project) and textured with procedurally generated, numpy-authored PNG images embedded directly in the `.usdz` (worn/scratched paint, brushed metal with rust speckle, tire tread, dark trim) rather than flat colors — still not final production surfacing (no hand-painted or photo-sourced textures, no blend shapes), but no longer flat-shaded either. It exists so the RealityKit bridge can load a real asset file instead of code-generated marker entities while final art is produced; replacing it with production meshes (below) should not require any simulation or bridge changes, by design.

**Geometry/material quality pass**: the body shell and greenhouse are now Catmull-Rom-resampled from their control-point cross-sections (not raw linear facets between ~10 points) and smooth-shaded, giving rounded panel curvature instead of a faceted look. Wheels have real 5-spoke rims (hub + barrel + radiating spokes) instead of a blank disc. The paint material is a dark gunmetal clearcoat (`Coat Weight`/`Coat Roughness` on the Principled BSDF) matching `ref_side.png`'s finish, rather than flat automotive paint. None of this closes the gap to the reference images' actual photorealism (hand-modeled panel gaps, physically accurate materials, proper environment/IBL lighting, post-processing) — that remains an artist-or-image-to-3D-tool problem — but it is materially closer than the earlier flat-primitive pass.

**Proportion calibration pass**: measured `ref_side.png`/`ref_front.png` directly with a pixel grid (overlaying gridlines on an upscaled crop and reading ground/beltline/roof-peak rows) rather than estimating by eye alone. This caught a real geometry bug — `GROUND` (ride height / wheel-center height) was 0.36 while the tire's outer radius was 0.475, so the wheels sank through the floor plane by ~0.12 units — fixed by raising `GROUND` to 0.50. It also showed the reference's greenhouse rises roughly as tall as the lower body below the beltline, well beyond the previous pass's shallow roof bump; `roof_z` raised from 0.84 to 1.00 accordingly, with the armor/cargo roof-mount offsets (literal `GROUND + <offset>` expressions) updated to still clear the now-taller roof. Note: a later, more careful re-reading of the beltline row on the same crop produced a noticeably lower greenhouse-height estimate than the one that drove this change, which is a useful caution about how sensitive this kind of manual pixel-grid reading is to a few pixels of misjudgment on a ~130px-tall crop — treat single-pass pixel-grid readings as approximate, not exact.

**Bug-fix pass**: rendered previews kept showing geometry visibly detached from the car body. Chasing this by re-guessing from pixel appearance alone produced two real-but-not-the-actual-bug fixes (the front splitter and rear diffuser fins/exhaust tips were genuinely poking past the tapered nose/tail, fixed by pulling them into the wider bumper regions) before the actual cause of the most visible floating piece was found by forward-projecting every object's world position to screen space and checking which one landed on it: `hoodVent` is parented to `body_panels` (which already translates by `(0, 0, GROUND)`), but its own location also added `GROUND + 0.475` on top of that, double-counting `GROUND` and placing it 0.5 units above the actual hood surface. Also fixed: the supercharger cylinder poked ~0.17 units above the hood with no scoop housing to justify it (lowered fully under the hood), and the grille/headlights were sized past where the actual (Catmull-Rom-smoothed) body surface supports them. General lesson: when a component is "parented to X" and X itself carries a `GROUND`-based offset, that component's own local offset must not repeat `GROUND` — and visually detached geometry is worth verifying against the actual mesh/world coordinates before trying a fix, since guessing from a render's pixel appearance is unreliable.

**Body-proportion pass (real-world calibration)**: the previous "stance calibration pass" set wheelbase/length to 0.696 by pixel-reading `ref_side.png` directly. That contradicts well-established real Mustang/Shelby GT500 dimensions (the reference car is clearly a modified GT500): wheelbase/length ~0.569, height/wheelbase ~0.508, wheel-diameter/wheelbase ~0.254 (2720mm wheelbase, 4784mm length, 1381mm height, ~691mm wheels). Those are authoritative and not subject to the noise a ~130px-tall crop introduces, so this pass treats the 0.696 photo reading as the error and recalibrates against the real-car ratios instead: rear wheel moved from x=-2.19 back to x=-1.48 (wheelbase/length now ~0.572, close to the 0.569 target; front wheel barely moved, it was already close), and the greenhouse raised further (`roof_z` from 1.00 to 1.18) so total vehicle height hits the 0.508 height/wheelbase target (~1.63 in this model's units, verified against the exported mesh, not just eyeballed). `body_sections`' rear fender-flare/decklid/rear-bumper x-positions, the suspension corners, and the armor/cargo roof-mount offsets were all updated to track the moved rear wheel and raised roof.

The wheelbase saga across these last few passes (original 0.549 → overcorrected to 0.696 from a flawed photo read → corrected to ~0.572 from real-car dimensions) is worth remembering: when a design is explicitly derived from a known real vehicle, that vehicle's published dimensions are a more reliable calibration source than pixel-measuring a low-resolution reference crop.

**Front/rear orthogonal check**: every proportion pass above had only been checked against the side profile and 3/4-angle renders. A true front-on and rear-on orthogonal render (matching how `ref_front.png`/`ref_rear.png` are actually framed) showed a real defect those angles hid: the `roof_z=1.18` height reached for the real-GT500 height/wheelbase ratio, combined with the greenhouse's `half_w=0.80` being much narrower than the body below it, made the greenhouse read as an isolated narrow fin/tower rather than an integrated roof. Comparing directly against `ref_front.png` showed the reference *does* have a similarly prominent trapezoidal windshield silhouette from straight-on (so the basic shape language was right), but its glasshouse spans nearly the full body width, and its side walls run much closer to vertical than the tapered "tent" walls this model had. Widened the greenhouse to `half_w~0.94-0.98` (from 0.80), reduced `roof_z` to 0.98 (a compromise short of the real-ratio-derived 1.18, since the front/rear silhouette is what visibly broke and no single cross-section height satisfies both the side-profile ratio and the front-view balance), and changed the greenhouse side-wall taper factor in `green_ring()` from 0.92 to 0.98 (nearly vertical, was reading as a pyramid). Also widened and moved the taillights outward to match `ref_rear.png`'s wide corner clusters (they were small and centrally bunched before). **Lesson**: verify a symmetric shape like a car body from all the angles it's actually meant to be seen from, not just the one or two views already being checked — a fix that looks right in profile can still be wrong head-on.

**Detailing pass (explicitly a blockout-tier ceiling, not a push toward photorealism)**: the project owner asked to get this "way closer to 90-100%" match against the reference photos. That is not achievable by iterating further on this scripted primitive/loft approach — the gap to those photos is categorical (sculpted continuous surfaces, UV-painted or photo-sourced materials, modeled optics, panel gaps, environment/IBL lighting) not a matter of more parameter tuning, and this was communicated directly rather than attempting to chase a number this method cannot reach. What this pass does move: added round lamp lenses in a housing for both headlights and taillights (were flat boxes with no lamp shape), rearranged the taillight lenses into a 2x2 grid to match `ref_rear.png`'s cluster shape (was a single vertical stack), added a crossed grille grid (vertical uprights added to the existing horizontal slats, closer to a woven mesh than louvers), added side mirrors (missing entirely before), and added a bevel modifier to the greenhouse to round its edges instead of leaving them knife-sharp. A procedural sky environment + bright emissive "studio panel" reflectors was also tried, to give the clearcoat paint something more interesting to reflect than a flat background color; at any workable strength it blew the whole render out to near-white, washing out the dark gunmetal paint tone that was already correct, so it was reverted rather than shipped as a regression — the flat background from the previous pass stayed. None of this changes the fundamental blockout-vs-production-art gap described above.

**Stance calibration pass**: measured wheelbase/length and wheel-diameter/length ratios against `ref_side.png` using clearer landmarks (wheel-circle centers, nose/tail extremes) than the ambiguous beltline row. Reference wheelbase/length ≈ 0.696 and wheel-diameter/length ≈ 0.110; this model's wheels at x=1.35/-1.75 gave only 0.549 (both overhangs much longer than the reference's short-overhang fastback stance), and the tires (outer radius 0.475) gave ≈0.168 (oversized). Moved the wheels to x=1.75/-2.19 (wheelbase/length now ≈0.697) with the front/rear fender-flare control points in `body_sections` and the suspension corners tracking the new positions, and reduced tire/rim/hub/spoke/rotor/caliper dimensions ~10% (with `GROUND` lowered to 0.45 to match) rather than closing the wheel-size gap all the way, to keep the "big wheels" character the visual canon calls for while still meaningfully closing the gap.

## Required entity hierarchy

`XR13_Assembly` → `chassis` → `bodyPanels`, `engineBay`, `powertrain`, `transmission`, `suspension`, `wheels`, `cabin`, `dashboard`, `armor`, `cargo`. The stable IDs are defined by `DHRev10AssetManifest` and the RealityKit scene bridge.

## Modeling requirements

Export separate meshes for the chassis, Boss 429-pattern block, Roots supercharger, radiator, DCT housing, suspension arms, wheels, dashboard needles, warning lights, armor, and cargo. Use blend shapes for body-panel deformation rather than crash-time model swaps.

## Audio requirements

Supply isolated looping stems for exhaust, valvetrain, and supercharger; transient clips for DCT shifts, radiator hiss, engine knock, and seizure; and radio consequence stems. `KingmakerAudioMixState` provides the runtime mix parameters.

## Procedural presentation

Wheel rotation, steering, suspension compression, dashboard needles, fault lights, deformation weights, thermal emission, and spatial-audio mix are driven from Swift state. Reality Composer Pro should assemble the authored hierarchy and colliders; it should not duplicate simulation logic.

The detailed modeling, animation, deformation, crafting, companion, and weather requirements are catalogued in `ResearchLibrary/KINGMAKER_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE_MATRIX.md`.

## Reality Composer Pro handoff

Create `XR13_Assembly`, import the separated USDZ parts, preserve the stable node names, add simplified body collision and primitive wheel/engine colliders, then load the scene through the existing `DHRev10RealityKitScene` bridge.

## Historical iteration log (continued)

Regenerated through the now-parameterized `build_kingmaker.py` (`--output-dir`) rather than a session scratchpad. Verified against `swift build`/`swift test` after each change and cross-checked every new/moved part's position against the actual exported mesh surface (not just re-rendering and eyeballing) before shipping, per the lesson from the earlier bug-fix pass. Still blockout-tier; this closes detail gaps, not the fundamental sculpted-surface-vs-lofted-blockout gap described above.
